Hello folks! The last 2 weeks have been an absolute whirlwind regarding Tunevia development, and I am beginning to get very excited! I’m happy to say that I have completed some of the major tasks mentioned in previous posts, and I will describe those and more to you now.

Taming the yt-dlp Memory Beast
Wow, that took a lot longer than I initially thought it would! The amount of RAM the YouTube downloader was eating in the background was much higher than I realized, making performance testing really difficult. It’s a lot easier to code something when you know exactly what data you actually need ahead of time! I thought I did, but it became clear shortly after debugging the new 1.6.3 update that yt-dlp was caching gigabytes of subtitle formats and thumbnails that we literally never use. The need for brainstorming got bad enough that I ended up working on this memory stripping logic for a few hours at a time before moving onto other things so as to make sure I continued being productive rather than stuck in my mind for long periods at a time trying to figure out how this complicated network request system should work. I finally figured it out and started stripping dictionaries to bare bones, before realizing during testing that I’d forgot yet another element during the brainstorming: the History panel was also leaking memory! That required me to spend at least a couple more hours redoing a significant portion of the code to ensure UI panels are actually destroyed rather than just hidden. The difficulty on this one was much higher than originally anticipated, but I am confident that every user is going to find it extraordinarily worth it once they see Tunevia sipping RAM like a lightweight champion.

Improvements regarding the Lyrics Panel and UI lag
So as many of these improvements were made literally earlier today, my testing data is a little sparse still and the degree to which improvements in this area are required at this point is going to remain somewhat unknown for the next couple of days as I test. However, today significant efforts were made to improve several issues regarding the lyrics panel getting stuck or out of sync with the audio player. I’m pretty sure though that I did succeed in my mission for today which was to get the UI to stop freezing sometimes when seeking backward in tracks. If you remember in the last post I mentioned fixing my profiler so that I could see how long certain functions were taking to execute. I saw that the code that synced the lyrics was taking way longer than it should to execute, so I finally recoded this the way I intended using an event based broadcasting system, rather than the timer I was using before that was checking the audio position every few hundred milliseconds. I’m not entirely sure why the audio engine can request hundreds of events a second and not lag and then the lyrics code can take so much time to execute only checking every 500ms, but it’s a thing I guess for some reason I don’t yet fully understand and so I recoded that logic with noticeable improvements. It’s hard to exactly measure stability as a whole, but there is 0 doubt that it has improved between the last post and this one!

Other minor improvements
As always, there were a bunch of smaller things improved upon that don’t require their own section in this post. As mentioned many times the old volume persistence on Tunevia was a mess putting it kindly, and I want to make absolutely certain I do it right this time which of course requires making it as easy as possible for users to skip tracks without getting blasted with 100% volume. This time though it’s not enough to deserve its own section. 

Aside from that stuff, I’ve just got a giant list of random smaller fixes and improvements. You guys are about to see the effectiveness of this bug reporting system, I have enough items here that I may as well put them in a list instead of a few wordy paragraphs. Well lets go, in the order presented to me by the unresolved bugs list.

* Pressing ctrl+f in the discovery panel was not focusing the search bar, but was instead bringing up a random menu. Now the keystroke for searching takes higher priority again like it should.

* Spacebar play/pause toggling was a little bit broken when you were focused in certain text boxes. Now it works flawlessly across all panels!
* apparently some local `.wav` files were not being detected by the Radar panel! Oops? We figured it out and it's working flawlessly now.
* The video comments dialog occasionally displayed raw HTML instead of parsed text for certain special symbols. The text parser handles those edge cases elegantly now.

Fine-Tuning the Details: The Small Section Polish
We also spent extra time polishing the subtle, day-to-day interactions across Tunevia to make sure everything feels human, thoughtful, and accessible.

First up, the Related Videos list is finally back in action! Whenever you search for something or play a song, Tunevia now fills the related items list with fresh recommendations right away instead of leaving you with a blank screen.

The Equalizer now remembers all of your settings properly. If you adjust the sliders or pick a preset like Rock or Pop, closing and reopening the dialog will keep your values intact instead of resetting back to Flat.

For screen reader users who prefer complete peace and quiet, we added a dedicated "Off" option to the NVDA verbosity settings. When you turn it off, Tunevia stays completely silent. We also made fast-forwarding, rewinding, and switching tabs quiet in default mode, so you will not hear repeated chatter while moving through your music. Furthermore, the Settings sidebar no longer pulls your keyboard focus away while you browse through categories with the arrow keys. You can comfortably press Tab or Enter when you are ready to explore a page.

The Downloader panel has been neatly simplified into a single clean list view, stripping away the duplicate log box and any awkward technical bracket codes. Download progress is now described in plain English, including helpful estimates like "about 2m left". You can also copy video links directly from the History tab using the new Copy Link button, the context menu, or by pressing Control+C.

Lastly, cookie files are now automatically detected from your Tunevia folder and stay saved permanently without repeated prompts, and the Tab Management screen now politely hides tabs for any features you have turned off in the Feature Manager.

The Great Stats Panel Minimization Situation
Ok so if you’re short on time or are just looking for serious content, feel free to skip to the next heading. But before we get into the tasks remaining sections, I wanted to pause for a not at all serious but hopefully amusing interlude.

I was working on fixing the Stats panel "total listening time" logic about a week ago. I mostly made a huge breakthrough, er… I thought I totally did at the time. So I published the patch to my local test build and went to relax for a bit. When I came back to check on things a few hours later, what I found was absolutely hilarious! I’d accidentally made a slight miscalculation when minimizing the app to the system tray. The stats panel just flat out stopped counting time entirely! I had essentially paused the flow of time for the entire application whenever it was out of sight. I ended up listening to an entire 3-hour podcast, opened the app back up, and the stats panel proudly declared I had been listening for exactly 14 seconds. It’s certainly one of those experiences I'm going to remember, especially since it’s one of those unique bugs that are actually quite funny. Anyway, this latest fluke seemed like a perfect opportunity to share a few quite entertaining moments from behind the scenes with all of you quick. Right, so now that you've hopefully gotten a small chuckle or grin, lets get back to what you all came for.

Version 1.6.5
	Added Spatial Audio so you can widen the stereo soundstage and add room depth from the Spatial Audio dialog (Ctrl+Shift+E) or toggle it with Ctrl+Shift+S. Pick between five presets or adjust Width, Room Size, and Depth directly.

	Fixed packaged builds failing to launch on other Windows PCs by bundling yt-dlp, lyricsgenius, BeautifulSoup, and ytmusicapi directly, preloading libvlccore.dll, and dropping machine-specific plugin caches.

	Fixed undefined size helpers in the Downloader, missing imports in the Audio Player, Lyrics, and Radar panels, empty-list crashes in Favorites and History, Light theme background resets, and seek intervals up to 1 hour.

Version 1.7.0
	Added a built-in update checker and updater (Help -> Check for Updates), fixed Alt+Tab leaving the window hidden or minimized, upgraded DSP processing to 48 kHz, and added local lossless file support (FLAC, WAV, AIFF, WavPack, APE, ALAC), Bass Boost, Vocal Clarity, Night Mode, NVDA Ducking, A-B Loop, Sleep Timer, Up-Next Queue, 0-9 jumps, and track info on the I key.

Version 1.7.1
	Extended Lossless mode (Ctrl+Shift+L) to YouTube streams, picking the highest available bitrate and reading out live codec, bitrate, and sample rate when you press I.

Version 1.7.4
	Audio effects (Spatial Audio, Bass Boost, Vocal Clarity, Night Mode, Mono, Normalization, Equalizer, and NVDA Ducking) now work while Lossless mode is enabled.

	Toggling or adjusting effects applies right away in real time without stopping or restarting playback.

	Vocal Clarity and Spatial Audio can now run together, and toggling Equalizer in Feature Manager updates the active track right away.
